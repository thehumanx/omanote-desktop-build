# Public links

Sometimes you want to show someone a set of links, notes, or todos without giving them an account or access to the rest of your canvas. omanote lets you share a **folder**, or a whole **canvas page**, as a public link.

## Sharing a folder

From a bookmark category, note folder, or todo folder, turn on sharing to get a public link. Anyone with the link can view that folder's contents in a clean read-only page — no sign-in required.

## What viewers see

The shared page shows a **snapshot** of the folder's contents, published by you. Viewers see exactly what you shared and nothing else — none of your other folders, and no way to edit. For bookmark and note shares, you can choose whether links appear as **cards** or a compact **list**.

## Sharing a canvas page

Open a canvas page and use the share button in its top bar. Anyone with the link sees a read-only copy of the page — its text, checklist items (with their checked state), and images. Turning it off takes the link down immediately.

Once a page is shared, a **Copy** button appears next to the others — it copies the public link, not the page's own address (which only works for you, signed in).

## Privacy of shares

Only the folder or page you explicitly share is public, and only as the snapshot you pushed. The rest of your canvas stays encrypted and private.

There is one trade worth knowing about. Everything in omanote is normally encrypted on your device before it's stored, so nobody — including us — can read it without your passphrase. A public link can't work that way: a visitor has no passphrase, so the page would be unreadable. Turning a link on therefore stores that one folder's (or page's) snapshot **unencrypted**, and only that one. For a shared page, this includes any images in it — a decrypted copy is published alongside the text; the original stays encrypted either way.

Turning sharing off disables the link **and deletes the unencrypted copy**, images included. Re-enabling it publishes a fresh snapshot from your device.

## Views

Shared folders keep a simple view count so you can see whether your link is being opened.
